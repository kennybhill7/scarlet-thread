## PART 1 — THE COVENANT FRAMEWORK

### 1.1 Where each covenant is textually instituted (widely agreed, low controversy)

This location data is convergent across every tradition surveyed (Reformed/covenant theology, dispensational, progressive covenantal, and general-audience sources like GotQuestions/BibleProject). Institution chapter/verse is **not** the disputed part of covenant theology — see 1.2 for what actually is disputed.

### 1.2 Where traditions genuinely disagree: covenant *relationship*, not covenant *location*

The app's existing policy (per the task brief) is to disclose interpretive posture rather than present one framework as neutral. Below are three real, named evangelical traditions, each independently verified via search, with what they agree on and where they diverge.

**A. Covenant Theology (classic Reformed — e.g., Westminster Standards tradition, represented in modern form by outlets like Ligonier/Tabletalk)**
- Organizes all the historical covenants above under two (sometimes three) *theological* covenants that are not themselves separate biblical texts but a systematic-theology construct: the **Covenant of Redemption** (intra-Trinitarian, pretemporal), the **Covenant of Works** (Adam in Eden), and the **Covenant of Grace** (from Genesis 3:15 onward, of which Noahic/Abrahamic/Mosaic/Davidic/New are successive *administrations* of one underlying gracious covenant).
- Stresses strong continuity between Israel and the Church — "essentially the same...administrated differently" [5].
- Source: The Gospel Coalition's covenant essay explicitly attributes the covenant-of-redemption/works/grace framework to "Reformed/Covenant Theology" and notes internal scholarly disagreement even within that camp about how the three theological covenants relate [1][5].

**B. Dispensationalism**
- Organizes biblical history around distinct **dispensations** (administrations/eras) rather than a single unfolding covenant of grace.
- Maintains a **strong, enduring distinction between Israel and the Church** — Israel retains a future, literal (not merely typological) referent; the covenants made with Israel (Abrahamic, Mosaic, Davidic, New) are not treated as simply transferred to/fulfilled in the Church in the same way covenant theology reads them.
- Source: Christianity.com's dispensationalism-vs-covenant-theology overview and The Gospel Coalition's "Progressive Covenantalism and New Covenant Theology" essay, both independently describing dispensationalism this way [6][7].

**C. Progressive Covenantalism** (a named, published mediating position — Stephen Wellum & Brent Parker, *Progressive Covenantalism: Charting a Course between Dispensational and Covenantal Theologies*)
- Explicitly positions itself as a third way: affirms **continuity** with covenant theology (Jesus and the Church fulfill/continue the OT storyline) but denies the covenants are "homogenous" the way covenant theology treats them (e.g., rejects that circumcision-of-infants maps directly onto infant baptism).
- Diverges from dispensationalism by treating "the land" promise as ultimately typological of new creation rather than requiring a future literal fulfillment tied to ethnic/national Israel.
- Source: The Gospel Coalition [7]; Andy Naselli's and Modern Reformation's independent reviews of the Wellum/Parker volume, both confirming this "mediating position" framing [8][9].

**Where they agree:** the five covenants' textual locations (1.1 table), their basic content (promise/land/seed for Abrahamic; law/conditionality for Mosaic; dynasty for Davidic; forgiveness/inward transformation for New), and that the New Covenant is inaugurated by Christ and expounded in Hebrews.

**Where they genuinely, currently disagree (not resolved by more research — these are live, published positions among serious evangelical scholars):** (a) whether the covenants are best modeled as one unified "covenant of grace" with administrative variations, or as distinct/discontinuous dispensations, or as a "genuinely new but continuous" progressive unfolding; (b) whether OT Israel and the NT Church are the same entity under different administration, two distinct entities, or a typological relationship; (c) whether OT land/nation promises to Israel require a future literal fulfillment or are fulfilled/transposed into Christ and the new creation.

**Recommendation for the app:** the covenant rail should ship the 1.1 table (institution location + basic content) as fact, and should NOT assert a position on 1.2 (relationship/continuity) without an explicit "interpretive lens" disclosure, consistent with the app's stated policy of not presenting one framework as neutral.

## PART 2 — KINGS & PROPHETS TIMELINE (Divided Monarchy → Exile → Return)

### 2.1 The chronology problem and its standard scholarly solution

The regnal-year numbers given in Kings/Chronicles for the two kingdoms don't trivially reconcile when read as simple consecutive totals — this is a genuine, long-documented puzzle, not a reader-confusion myth. **Edwin R. Thiele's *The Mysterious Numbers of the Hebrew Kings* (1951, based on his doctoral dissertation)** is confirmed by independent sources as the standard reference conservative/evangelical scholarship and study Bibles use to reconcile these numbers [10][11][12].

**Thiele's solution, in brief** (confirmed via Wikipedia's article on the book, cross-checked against a Themelios review by McFall):
- **Different regnal-year counting conventions between the two kingdoms**: Judah used *accession-year* reckoning (the partial first year on the throne doesn't count as "year 1"); Israel, for periods of its history, used *non-accession-year* reckoning (the partial first year counts as "year 1"), which by itself creates a one-year-per-transition drift between the two kingdoms' numbers if not corrected for [11].
- **Different new-year calendar starts**: Judah's regnal year began in Tishri (autumn); Israel's began in Nisan (spring) — a six-month offset that further scrambles naive synchronisms [11][13].
- **Co-regencies**: several kings reigned jointly with a father/predecessor for a period (e.g., overlapping reigns), which Thiele used to resolve remaining discrepancies. This is the single most-contested piece of his method — critics note the co-regencies are not always independently attested outside the numbers-reconciliation itself [11][12].
- Thiele anchored the whole system to two external, non-biblical fixed points: the Battle of Qarqar (853 BC) and Shalmaneser III's Black Obelisk (841 BC, referencing Jehu), which are dated from Assyrian records independent of the biblical text [11].

**Current scholarly status (confidence: reconstructed, not settled beyond dispute, but the leading framework):**
- Thiele's dates became the reference standard — Assyriologist D. J. Wiseman is quoted as saying "the chronology most widely accepted today is one based on the meticulous study by Thiele" [11].
- A 2005 Themelios review by Leslie McFall (a scholar who has published extensively refining Thiele's system) frames the state of the field as "a qualified yes" that the chronology is settled — dates are narrowed to within about a year of a fixed position — but explicitly still calls Thiele's system "only an hypothesis," and identifies three competing schools of thought: **Harmonists** (Thiele's own camp — trust the Hebrew text, reconcile via reckoning/calendar/co-regency devices), **Restorationists** (assume scribal corruption, repair the text using variants from the Septuagint/Dead Sea Scrolls/Josephus), and **Reconstructionists** (assume the numbers reflect an artificial theological/schematic pattern imposed by editors, requiring wholesale reconstruction — associated with scholars like Jeremy Hughes) [12].
- Named critics (e.g., Gershon Galil) argue Thiele's repeated shifts in reckoning basis amount to *ad hoc* / circular reasoning tailored to make the numbers work rather than demonstrated historical practice [10][11].
- Later scholars — **Leslie McFall, Rodger Young**, and others — have published refinements/extensions of Thiele rather than replacements, and Young in particular has published peer-reviewed defenses and extensions of the Thiele framework (e.g., in JETS) [10][14].
- **Bottom line for the app:** treat exact BC year boundaries as **historical_context / inference**, not text_explicit — they are a defensible, widely-used scholarly reconstruction (Thiele's), not something Scripture states in Arabic-numeral BC dates, and real scholars differ by a few years at various points and by method more broadly.

### 2.4 Persian-period kings, for the post-exilic tail of the "Israel" stage

Since the monarchy ends in 586 BC but the app's stage 5 runs through Malachi, the timeline rail needs foreign (Persian) kings, not Judahite ones, for this tail:
- **Cyrus the Great** — decree permitting return, 538 BC
- **Darius I** — reigned c. 521–486 BC; Haggai and Zechariah (chs. 1–8) dated to his 2nd year, 520 BC; the rebuilt temple completed in his reign, 515 BC
- **Xerxes I (Ahasuerus)** — reigned c. 485–465 BC (the king of the book of Esther, not in the prophet list above but relevant to the same era)
- **Artaxerxes I** — reigned c. 464–424/423 BC; Ezra's return (458 BC) and Nehemiah's mission (445 BC) both fall under him

## PART 3 — WHAT'S SAFE TO SHIP AS FACT VS. WHAT NEEDS DISCLOSED-UNCERTAINTY FRAMING

Using the app's own text_explicit / historical_context / inference / canonical_synthesis / tradition vocabulary as a grading rubric (my own judgment on the grading, not sourced from the app's internal docs):

**Safe to ship as `text_explicit` fact (directly stated in the biblical text, not seriously disputed across traditions):**
- The five covenants' institution chapters (1.1 table) — every citation there is a direct textual location.
- Which kings are named as reigning during a prophet's ministry, wherever the book's own superscription says so directly: Isaiah 1:1 (Uzziah–Hezekiah), Micah 1:1, Hosea 1:1, Amos 1:1, Zephaniah 1:1, Jeremiah 1:1-3, Haggai/Zechariah's dating to Darius, Ezekiel's dating method (by Jehoiachin's exile-years), Ezra/Nehemiah's dating to Cyrus/Artaxerxes.
- That covenant theology, dispensationalism, and progressive covenantalism are three real, distinctly named, currently-live evangelical positions (not the app's invention) — each is independently documented by multiple sources above.
- That a real chronological puzzle exists in reconciling Kings/Chronicles regnal years, and that Thiele's 1951 work is the standard reference for resolving it in conservative/evangelical scholarship.

**Needs `historical_context` or `inference` framing (real, well-sourced, but reconstructed rather than textually stated):**
- Every specific BC year in the kings table (2.2) and every "narrow window" BC range for a prophet's ministry (2.3) — these are Thiele's (or an alternative scholar's) reconstruction, not numbers Scripture states in BC terms. Recommend the app label these explicitly as "BC dates per Thiele's chronology" (or whichever source is used) rather than presenting them as bare fact, and ideally footnote that a different reputable chronology (e.g., Bright's) shifts the whole table by up to ~9 years.
- Co-regency assumptions embedded in the kings table (e.g., Jotham/Uzziah, Jotham's dates overlapping Uzziah's) — these are part of Thiele's specific solution to the numbers problem and are the most-contested piece of his method per McFall's own review.
- Where a prophet's date/king is inferred purely from internal evidence rather than a named superscription: Nahum, Habakkuk, Malachi, Ezekiel's underlying 597 BC anchor.

**Needs explicit `tradition`/disputed framing (real, unresolved disagreement even among serious scholars/traditions — do not present a single answer):**
- Obadiah's date (9th century vs. 6th century candidates, genuinely unresolved).
- Joel's date (9th century vs. c. 400 BC, genuinely unresolved, tied to a live critical-vs-conservative methodological split).
- Any claim about how the five covenants *relate* to one another (covenant of grace vs. dispensational distinction vs. progressive covenantalism's middle position) — per the task brief's explicit instruction, the app should not adjudicate this.
- Any claim that Abrahamic/Davidic promises are "fulfilled" specifically at a particular later stage (e.g., stage 11/Paradise Restored) rather than in Christ generally — this is downstream of the same covenant-relationship dispute.

**Recommended UI pattern:** covenant rail badges for stage institution-locations = plain fact display; timeline rail king/prophet badges = plain fact display where the superscription is explicit; BC year ranges throughout = display with a small "per Thiele's chronology" attribution or tooltip, since that's an honest one-line disclosure that doesn't require re-litigating the whole debate in-app.
