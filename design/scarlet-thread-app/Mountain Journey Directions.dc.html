Scarlet Thread · The Mountain, reimagined · turn 1

Three ways to make the mountain a place

All three keep the real data model exactly as it is — 11 stages, ascent 1–6 to the peak at Gospels, descent 6–11, curated mirror pairs, per-stage observationCount / questionCount / threadCount driving visual weight. What differs is the camera: where you stand relative to the terrain, and therefore what "traveling from Genesis to Revelation" physically feels like. Each is buildable as layered SVG + CSS transforms on a mid-range phone.

Token bug, not inherited

None of these use var(--crimson). The shell needs its own fixed value: --shell-crimson: #d9615f, declared in the :root shell layer beside --gold and never touched by [data-reading]. Measured 5.12:1 against --shell-bg #0d1420 — clears AA for normal text, so it is safe for the thread and for its labels. The parchment value #a13333 measures 2.67:1 on the shell and must never reach it.

Curated vs. learner, kept legible

Proposal for the open question: the scarlet thread is the ground — a road you walk, curated, identical for everyone. A learner's own UserConnection is never ground. It is a thin hand-stitched filament in the air above the terrain, with the perpendicular ticks from the earlier Stitched Thread sketch. Walkable vs. stitched — two different physical categories, unmistakable even at thumbnail size.

Where the cinema comes from

Not GPU effects. Four things a slow phone can do: atmospheric perspective (distant ranges lighter and hazier), a sky that shifts hue across the journey, 3–5 parallax layers on translate3d only, and the thread drawing itself ahead of you via stroke-dashoffset. No per-frame JS, no canvas, no video. Everything below degrades to a static image under prefers-reduced-motion.

1a

The Switchback

You stand in the valley and look up. Scrolling up is climbing: the terrain slides down past you, the ranges behind move slower, the peak stays high and far. The thread is a switchback road cut into the face — you can see the whole route above you and how much is left, which is the emotional core of "front to back."

Data mapping. Distance along the road is proportional to the stage's real chapter count — so Gen 12–Malachi is a long, lonely traverse and the Gospels arrive suddenly at the top. Cairn size comes from threadCount; gold-filled means observationCount > 0, hollow means unwritten. Labels resolve only for reached stages plus the next one; the rest stay bare markers.

The peak is the midpoint, not the end. Past the Gospels the same scroll keeps going and you are descending the far face — sky warming toward Revelation's morning, ranges reversing direction. Mirror pairs read as matching altitude across the two faces. The ribbon at the bottom is the whole 11-stage arc, always visible.

Risk. A tall scroll surface on a phone is a lot of pixels of terrain to author, and the switchback geometry has to be generated from stage lengths rather than hand-drawn, which is real work.

1b

The Traverse

You see the whole range side-on and travel across it. The mountain is one continuous panorama, roughly four screens wide, dragged horizontally. Both faces are in frame at once, so the mirror structure is spatially obvious without a single dashed tie line — Creation and Paradise Restored simply sit at the same altitude on opposite sides.

Time-travel is in the light. The sky gradient runs left-to-right across the full panorama: cold blue-black at Genesis, warm brass at the peak, a red-gold dawn at Revelation. You do not read that as a chart axis — you feel the hours passing as you drag. Four parallax layers at different rates give the depth.

Cheapest to build and cheapest to run. One fixed-height SVG band, one horizontal scroll container, layers on translate3d(x,0,0). No generated geometry: the terrain can be hand-authored once because it never changes. The panel below the range is the existing tooltip promoted into a real card, with the mirror always shown.

Risk. Horizontal drag inside a vertically-scrolling app needs care, and the mountain reads a little more as a diorama than a place you are inside — the least "cinematic" of the three, in exchange for being the most legible.

1c

On the Road

You are standing on the thread. The road comes out from under your feet at the bottom of the screen and runs away into the distance toward the peak. This is the most literal reading of the Yellow Brick Road: not a route you inspect, a route you are on. The scarlet is unmissable because it occupies the foreground.

Travel = the road's texture moving. Scrolling advances you: the weave rungs slide toward the bottom of the screen and grow, the peak grows fractionally, foreground rock walls sweep past. Waypoints arrive from the vanishing point, pass you, and are gone. Cheap — it is one translate on a repeating rung group.

Cost of the camera. From on the road you cannot see the whole 11-stage structure or the mirror pairs at all — that is the trade for immersion. It needs a companion overview (1b's panorama makes a good "map" gesture from here, so these two are not necessarily rivals).

Risk. A faked perspective corridor is the easiest of the three to make look cheap or video-game-ish, and it is the furthest from the app's quiet, contemplative register. It lives or dies on restraint in the sky and the haze.

Where I'd go, and what I need from you

1a is the strongest single answer to the brief as written — it is a real mountain, the thread is genuinely the road, and the peak-as-midpoint gives the Genesis-to-Revelation shape a physical meaning that no chart can. 1b is the safest and would ship soonest, and its side-on panorama is the only direction where the curated mirror pairs stay visible for free. 1c is the biggest emotional swing and the biggest risk.

Three things would let me commit: does the peak sit at the Gospels as the frontmatter says, or does Creation belong at the top (the open elevationOf() question in PROGRESS.md)? Should the Mountain be the same surface a learner navigates from daily, or a place they visit on Sundays to see the whole shape? And do you want a learner's own connections on this surface at all, or held back for the Review screen?
