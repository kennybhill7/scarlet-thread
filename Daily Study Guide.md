# START HERE — How to run this every day

This vault is already seeded with the eleven stages of the mountain diagram, ten thread notes,
and templates. Nothing here is finished. It's scaffolding. You fill it.

---

## One-time setup (about ten minutes)

1. Install Obsidian (free) on your phone and your laptop.
2. Put the `Bible-Brain` folder in iCloud Drive, then in Obsidian choose **Open folder as vault**
   and point it there. Do this on both devices and they'll stay in sync.
3. In Settings → Files & Links, turn on **Use [[Wikilinks]]** and set the template folder to
   `99 Templates`.
4. Turn on the **Templates** core plugin and the **Daily notes** core plugin. Point daily notes at
   `05 Log` and set its template to `05 Log/_Daily Template`.
5. Open the graph view once. It'll look sparse. That's correct — you haven't studied yet.

That's the whole setup. Resist adding plugins for at least a month.

---

## The daily loop — 25 minutes

**1. Read first, with the phone face down. (10 min)**
Paper Bible if you have one. No notes yet. You are not studying to produce anything at this stage,
you're listening. If your first move is to open an app, the app is running you.

**2. Open the passage note and write three lines under Observation. (5 min)**
Not summaries — observations. Something you actually noticed. "The serpent quotes God inaccurately
and she corrects him by adding to it too" is an observation. "Genesis 3 is about the fall" is a
summary, and it's worthless to you a year from now.

**3. Link one thread. (2 min)**
This is the step that builds the brain, and it's the step everyone skips. Type `[[` and pick a
thread, or make a new one. Then go into that thread note and add your one line under
*Where it shows up*. Both directions. Every time.

**4. Write one question under Questions. (3 min)**
Real ones. The things that bother you. A vault full of answers is a dead vault; a vault full of
live questions pulls you back tomorrow.

**5. Daily log entry. (5 min)**
One sentence on what you saw. The question you're carrying. Then pray it. Close the laptop.

If you only have ten minutes, do steps 1 and 3. Reading plus one link still grows the thing.

## Where the doodling goes

Ink in the Notes app, links in Obsidian.

- Sketch in Notes — the mountain, a timeline, a passage marked up, whatever your hand wants to do.
- Title that note the same as the Obsidian note: `Gen 03-05 — Sin Enters`.
- Long-press the note in the list → **Copy Link**.
- Paste it under `## Ink` in the matching Obsidian note.

Now the drawing is one tap away and you never had to make Obsidian do something it's bad at.
Diagrams, charts, and anything spatial should live in ink. Trying to type a diagram is how people
end up with beautiful notes they never look at again.

---

## When to make a new thread note

Make one the third time you notice something. Not the first — the first time is a coincidence, and
if you make a note every time something catches your eye you'll have four hundred stubs by August.

When you do make one: one-line definition at the top, then every place you've seen it, then leave
it alone. Threads are supposed to sit half-empty for months.

Signs it's a real thread: it appears on both sides of the mountain, or a New Testament writer
explicitly reaches back for it (Peter with the flood, Paul with Abraham).

## The weekly rhythm

Work the mirror pairs, one per week, Old Testament first:

| Week | Read first | Then |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | [[Gen 01-02 — Creation]] | [[Rev 20-22 — Paradise Restored]] |
| 2 | [[Gen 03-05 — Sin Enters]] | [[Rev 20 — Satan Cast Out]] |
| 3 | [[Gen 06-09 — The Flood]] | [[Rev 06-19 — The World Judged]] |
| 4 | [[Gen 10-11 — Babel]] | [[Rev 01-18 — Babylon]] |
| 5 | [[Gen 12–Malachi — Israel]] | [[Acts–Jude — The Church]] |
| 6 | [[Gospels — Jesus Christ]] | the whole climb again, in one sitting |

Read the Old Testament side Monday through Wednesday and sit with it. Don't jump to the answer.
The waiting is doing something to you that the answer can't.

Six weeks gets you the map. Then you start over at Genesis 1 and go verse by verse, and now every
chapter has somewhere to hang.

---

## Sunday review — 20 minutes, non-negotiable

1. Open **Graph view**. Look for a thread note with a lot of lines running into it. That's where
   God has been working on you this month. It's usually not the one you expected.
2. Open the two or three thread notes you touched. Read your own lines back. Add anything under
   *What I'm seeing*.
3. Find one thing worth teaching. Start a note in `04 Teaching`. Even one line.
4. Look for orphans — notes with no links. Either connect them or delete them.

Step 3 is the one that turns study into leadership. What you don't give away, you lose.

---

## When you fall behind

You will. Don't restart, don't rebuild the system, don't reorganize the folders. Reorganizing is
what studying feels like when you don't want to study.

Just open today's passage and write one observation. The vault doesn't care that you missed
eleven days.

---

## The rule that holds it together

**Every passage note links to at least one thread note. Every thread note links back.**

That's it. That's the whole system. Everything else here is optional.
